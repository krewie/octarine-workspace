# 将　ショウ

Core idea: lead / command / something that is about to come

将来　[しょうらい]

future

> 将来、日本に住みたい。
>
> しょうらい、にほん　に　すみたい。
>
> I want to live in Japan in the future.

Other words:

- 将軍 [しょうぐん]　shogun / military commander
- 対象　たいしょう　general, leader, chief

将 alone does not normally mean “Future”. That meaning comes from compound 将来。

# 捕る【とる】

## Core meaning: catch / capture

**捕る** is pronounced **とる**. It shares its pronunciation with several other verbs, but the kanji shows what kind of “taking” is meant.

## 捕る

**捕る** focuses on catching, capturing, or trapping something, especially something that moves.

> 魚を捕る。
> さかなをとる。
> Catch fish.

> 虫を捕る。
> むしをとる。
> Catch an insect.

For catching a person, criminal, or animal, **捕まえる【つかまえる】** is generally more common:

> 警察が犯人を捕まえた。
> けいさつがはんにんをつかまえた。
> The police caught the criminal.

---

## 取る【とる】

**取る** is the broad, ordinary verb meaning “take,” “get,” or “obtain.”

> 本を取る。
> ほんをとる。
> Take the book.

> 資格を取る。
> しかくをとる。
> Obtain a qualification.

It is the default kanji when no more specific kind of “taking” needs to be expressed.

---

## 獲る【とる】

**獲る** focuses on successfully acquiring prey, game, a catch, or something won through effort.

> 獲物を獲る。
> えものをとる。
> Capture prey.

> 大物を獲る。
> おおものをとる。
> Catch a large animal or fish.

> 金メダルを獲る。
> きんメダルをとる。
> Win a gold medal.

The distinction between **捕る** and **獲る** can overlap:

- **魚を捕る** — catch or capture fish

- **魚を獲る** — obtain fish as a catch

**捕る** emphasizes the act of catching.
**獲る** emphasizes successfully securing what was hunted or pursued.

---

## 撮る【とる】

**撮る** is used for photography, filming, and recording.

> 写真を撮る。
> しゃしんをとる。
> Take a photograph.

> 動画を撮る。
> どうがをとる。
> Record a video.

> 映画を撮る。
> えいがをとる。
> Shoot a film.

---

## Summary

- **取る** — take, get, obtain

- **捕る** — catch, capture, trap

- **獲る** — secure prey, a catch, or a prize

- **撮る** — photograph, film, record

All four verbs are pronounced **とる**, but their kanji specifies what kind of “taking” is meant.

# 如し　ごとし

Core idea: like / as / similar to

This is an old-fashioned or literary expression. In modern Japanease, it often appears as:

- **～の如し【～のごとし】** — is like…
- **～の如く【～のごとく】** — like…, as if…
- **～の如き【～のごとき】** — such as…, something like…

例えば

> 光陰矢の如し。
>
> こういんや　の　ごとし。
>
> Time flies like an arrow.
>
> it is literally “Time is like an arrow.”

> 風の如し走る。
>
> かぜ　の　ごとし　はしえる。
>
> Run like the wind.

In ordinary modern conversation, you would normally use:

- **～のようだ** — is like
- **～のように** — like / in the manner of
- **～みたいだ** — seems like / is like

Therefore:

> 彼は風のように走る。
>
> かれ　は　かぜ　の　ように　はしえる。
>
> He runs like the wind.

is ordinary, while:

> 彼は風の如しく走る。
>
> かれ　は　かぜ　の　ごとしく　はしる。

Sounds literary, dramatic or archaic.
